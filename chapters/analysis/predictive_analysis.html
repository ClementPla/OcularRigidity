9  Predictive analysis

In Chapter 8, each point is a visit and both variables are measured at the same time. Here we take a variable at the first visit and correlate it with the slope of another variable over the follow-up, in unit per year. Each point is therefore an eye and not a visit. We have ?var:pred.n_eyes eyes, with ?var:pred.visits_min to ?var:pred.visits_max visits each and a median follow-up of ?var:pred.median_followup years.

Progression in glaucoma has no good predictor, and the IOP, which is what we treat, is a weak one. So we keep the IOP in every comparison below and read the other variables against it. The question is not whether a variable predicts progression well, it is whether it does better than what we already rely on.

9.1 Slopes and follow-up

A slope computed on two visits a few months apart is not progression. Figure 9.1 shows the ocular rigidity at the first visit against the MD slope, with all the eyes on the left, and only the eyes with at least ?var:pred.min_visits visits over at least ?var:pred.min_span year on the right. The trend on the left is carried by the eyes with the shortest follow-up.

The worst MD slope, at ?var:pred.worst_md_slope dB/year, comes from an eye with ?var:pred.worst_md_visits visits over ?var:pred.worst_md_span year (Figure 9.2).

9.2 Comparing the predictors

Figure 9.8 puts every baseline variable against the four endpoints, with the IOP in red.

The IOP is flat on the three structural endpoints and only moves on the MD slope, where it goes the wrong way: a higher pressure at the first visit goes with a slower MD loss. That is what we would expect if the eyes with the highest pressure are also the ones whose treatment was adjusted.

Against that, the ocular rigidity matches the IOP on the MD slope and in the direction we expect, a stiffer eye losing MD faster (Figure 9.4). It also tracks the PSD and the BMO-MRW slopes, where the IOP does nothing.

9.3 Choroidal thickness

The choroidal thickness at the first visit gives the strongest trend of the table, on the BMO-MRW slope (Figure 9.6). It says nothing about the MD or the RNFL slope.

A thicker choroid at the first visit goes with a rim that thins more slowly (Figure 9.7). The change in choroidal thickness points the same way, slightly weaker. Both are well ahead of the IOP on this endpoint.

None of these trends is strong on its own, but they are overall more consistent than the IOP.
